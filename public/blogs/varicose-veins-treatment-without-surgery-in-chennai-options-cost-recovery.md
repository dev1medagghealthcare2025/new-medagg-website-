## What Are Varicose Veins?

Varicose veins are enlarged, twisted veins that commonly appear on the legs.

Healthy veins contain valves that help blood move upward toward the heart. When these valves become weak or stop functioning properly, blood can flow backwards and collect in the veins. This is known as venous reflux.

Over time, the affected veins can become enlarged and visible beneath the skin.

Some people have varicose veins without any symptoms. Others may experience:

* Aching or pain in the legs
* Leg heaviness
* Swelling around the ankles
* Itching
* Burning or throbbing sensations
* Muscle cramps
* Skin discolouration
* Skin changes around the ankle
* Bleeding from a varicose vein

Varicose veins can sometimes progress and lead to complications such as skin changes, superficial vein inflammation, bleeding or venous ulcers.


## Can Varicose Veins Be Treated Without Surgery?

Yes, many people with symptomatic varicose veins can be treated using minimally invasive procedures rather than traditional open surgery.

However, the right treatment depends on the underlying vein problem.

A specialist will assess the veins using duplex ultrasound, which helps identify abnormal blood flow and determine which veins are responsible for the symptoms. NICE recommends duplex ultrasound to confirm the diagnosis and assess the extent of venous reflux before treatment is planned.

Depending on the findings, treatment options may include:

* Endovenous Laser Ablation
* Radiofrequency Ablation
* Ultrasound-Guided Foam Sclerotherapy
* Additional minimally invasive treatment options may be considered in selected patients, depending on their vein anatomy and clinical findings.

Traditional surgery may still be appropriate for some patients when minimally invasive options are unsuitable.

The important point is that there is no single treatment that is suitable for everyone.


## Non-Surgical Treatment Options for Varicose Veins


### 1. Endovenous Laser Ablation

Endovenous Laser Ablation (EVLA/EVLT) is a minimally invasive treatment that uses laser energy to close an abnormal vein from the inside.

Instead of surgically removing the vein, a thin catheter or fibre is introduced into the affected vein, usually under ultrasound guidance. Laser energy is then used to heat and close the vein.

The body gradually reroutes blood through healthier veins.


### Potential advantages

* Minimally invasive
* No traditional vein stripping
* Small access point rather than a large surgical incision
* Usually performed with local anaesthesia
* Generally allows earlier mobilisation than traditional surgery

NICE categorises endovenous laser treatment as an endothermal ablation technique and recommends endothermal ablation as an interventional option for suitable patients with confirmed truncal reflux.


### 2. Radiofrequency Ablation

Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA) uses radiofrequency energy instead of laser energy.

A catheter is introduced into the affected vein and radiofrequency energy is used to heat the vein wall, causing the vein to close.

Like laser ablation, RFA is a minimally invasive approach and may be considered when the patient's vein anatomy and clinical findings make it appropriate.

The most appropriate treatment should be selected after an individual assessment, taking into account the patient’s vein anatomy, symptoms and clinical findings.


### 3. Ultrasound-Guided Foam Sclerotherapy

Foam sclerotherapy involves injecting a specially prepared sclerosant into an abnormal vein.

The procedure is performed under ultrasound guidance so that the specialist can accurately target the vein.

The injected foam causes the treated vein to close, after which the body gradually deals with the closed vein.

NICE recommends ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy when endothermal ablation is unsuitable for a patient with confirmed truncal reflux.

Foam sclerotherapy may also be used for certain smaller or tributary veins depending on the patient's condition.


## How Do Doctors Decide Which Treatment You Need?

You should not choose a varicose vein treatment based only on the appearance of the veins.

Two people can have veins that look similar on the outside but have completely different underlying problems.

The specialist may consider:

* The location of the affected veins
* The extent of venous reflux
* The size and anatomy of the veins
* Your symptoms
* Previous treatment
* Skin changes or ulcers
* Your overall health
* Whether one or both legs are affected
* Results of duplex ultrasound

This is why a duplex ultrasound assessment is an important part of treatment planning.


## What Happens During a Varicose Vein Evaluation?

Your consultation may involve:


### Step 1: Medical History

The specialist will ask about your symptoms, how long you have had them and whether they become worse after standing or walking.


### Step 2: Physical Examination

The legs may be examined for visible veins, swelling, skin changes and other signs of venous disease.


### Step 3: Duplex Ultrasound

A duplex ultrasound can evaluate blood flow through the veins and identify abnormal reflux.


### Step 4: Treatment Planning

Based on the findings, the specialist can explain whether treatment is required and which option may be appropriate.


### Step 5: Procedure

If an intervention is recommended, the treatment method will depend on the veins involved and your individual circumstances.

## "How much does varicose vein treatment cost in Chennai?"

There is no single price because treatment costs vary significantly between patients.

The cost can depend on:

* The treatment method
* One leg or both legs
* Number and size of veins treated
* Severity of venous disease
* Diagnostic tests required
* Specialist fees

* Anaesthesia requirements

* Follow-up care

Current Chennai pricing data published by Practo lists overall varicose vein treatment costs at approximately ₹1.04 lakh to ₹1.56 lakh, with an average around ₹1.27 lakh. Its listed figures for specific procedures also vary, including approximately ₹80,000–₹90,000 for one-leg EVLT and ₹1.25–₹1.35 lakh for two-leg EVLT. These are marketplace estimates rather than a fixed price for any particular clinic.

Your actual treatment cost can only be estimated accurately after assessing your condition and determining the appropriate procedure.

Before choosing a treatment centre, ask whether the quoted price includes:

* Consultation

## Is Non-Surgical Varicose Vein Treatment Better Than Surgery?

There is no universal answer.

For patients who are suitable candidates, minimally invasive treatments can provide an alternative to traditional vein surgery.

NICE recommends endothermal ablation for suitable patients with confirmed truncal reflux, followed by ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy when endothermal ablation is unsuitable, with surgery considered when those options are unsuitable.

The appropriate treatment should therefore be based on your vein anatomy, symptoms, ultrasound findings and medical history.


## Recovery After Non-Surgical Varicose Vein Treatment

Recovery depends on the procedure performed and the individual patient.

Many minimally invasive treatments are designed to reduce the need for prolonged recovery compared with traditional surgery.

Your specialist may advise you to:

* Walk soon after the procedure when appropriate
* Follow instructions regarding compression garments
* Take prescribed medicines if required
* Avoid strenuous activity temporarily
* Attend follow-up appointments
* Watch for unusual symptoms

The exact recovery timeline should be provided by your treating specialist because it varies according to the procedure and individual circumstances.

Some treatments may require compression stockings for a limited period after the procedure. NICE notes that compression may be used temporarily after interventional treatment, with specific duration depending on the clinical situation.


## When Should You See a Varicose Vein Specialist?

You should consider medical evaluation if your varicose veins are accompanied by:

* Persistent leg pain
* Leg swelling
* Significant heaviness or aching
* Itching or discomfort
* Skin colour changes
* Skin thickening or other skin changes
* A painful, hard superficial vein
* A venous ulcer
* Bleeding from a varicose vein

NICE recommends referral to a vascular service for symptomatic varicose veins and for complications such as skin changes, superficial vein thrombosis, venous ulcers or bleeding.

## Can Varicose Veins Come Back After Treatment?

Treatment closes or removes the problematic veins that are being treated, but it does not necessarily prevent new varicose veins from developing in the future.

NICE specifically advises that patients should be informed that new varicose veins may develop after treatment and that some patients may require more than one treatment session.

This is why follow-up and long-term management are important.

* Shorter recovery in many cases

However, these advantages do not mean that minimally invasive treatment is automatically the right choice for every patient.

The right procedure is the one that appropriately treats the underlying venous problem identified during assessment.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Varicose Veins Treatment Without Surgery


### 1. Can varicose veins be treated without surgery?

Yes. Depending on the underlying vein problem, minimally invasive treatments such as endovenous laser ablation, radiofrequency ablation and ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy may be considered. The appropriate treatment depends on the patient's clinical assessment and ultrasound findings.


### 2. What is the best non-surgical treatment for varicose veins?

There is no single best treatment for everyone. Endovenous laser treatment, radiofrequency ablation and foam sclerotherapy may be suitable for different types of venous disease. A duplex ultrasound can help determine the most appropriate treatment.


### 3. Is laser treatment for varicose veins really non-surgical?

Laser treatment is generally considered a minimally invasive procedure rather than traditional open surgery. A small access point is used to introduce a laser fibre into the vein.


### 4. How much does varicose vein treatment cost in Chennai?

The cost varies according to the treatment, number of legs treated, severity of the condition, diagnostic requirements and facility charges. Current Chennai marketplace estimates put overall treatment around ₹1.04 lakh–₹1.56 lakh, although individual procedure costs can differ considerably.


### 5. How much does laser treatment for varicose veins cost in Chennai?

The cost depends on whether one or both legs are treated and the specific procedure required. Practo currently lists approximately ₹80,000–₹90,000 for one-leg EVLT and ₹1.25–₹1.35 lakh for two-leg EVLT in Chennai. These are indicative marketplace figures, not a fixed No Surgeries price.


### 6. Do I need a Doppler or duplex ultrasound before treatment?

A duplex ultrasound is commonly used to confirm the diagnosis, assess venous reflux and plan treatment. NICE recommends duplex ultrasound for people being assessed for suspected primary or recurrent varicose veins.


### 7. Is varicose vein treatment painful?

The level of discomfort varies depending on the procedure and the individual. Minimally invasive treatments are generally performed using local anaesthesia or other appropriate pain-control methods. Your specialist should explain what you can expect before treatment.


### 8. How long does it take to recover after varicose vein treatment?

Recovery depends on the procedure and your individual condition. Minimally invasive treatments generally involve less recovery than traditional vein stripping, but your specialist will provide specific instructions about walking, work, exercise and compression.


### 9. Can I walk after varicose vein treatment?

Patients are often encouraged to mobilise after appropriate minimally invasive procedures, but the exact instructions depend on the treatment performed and your medical condition. Follow your treating specialist's advice.


### 10. Can varicose veins disappear permanently after treatment?

The treated vein can be closed or removed, but new varicose veins may develop over time. NICE recommends informing patients that new varicose veins can occur after treatment and that some patients may require additional treatment.


### 11. Can compression stockings cure varicose veins?

Compression stockings can help with symptoms in some patients, but they do not necessarily correct the underlying venous reflux. NICE recommends compression hosiery as a treatment when interventional treatment is unsuitable rather than as the routine permanent treatment when an intervention is appropriate.


### 12. Are varicose veins dangerous?

Many varicose veins are not dangerous and may cause little or no symptoms. However, some people can develop complications such as skin changes, bleeding, superficial vein thrombosis or venous ulcers. Symptomatic or complicated varicose veins should be assessed by an appropriate specialist.


### 13. When should varicose veins be treated?

Treatment may be considered when varicose veins cause troublesome symptoms or complications. A specialist assessment can determine whether treatment is medically appropriate and which option may be suitable.


### 14. Can varicose veins be treated without hospital admission?

Some minimally invasive varicose vein procedures can be performed without a prolonged hospital stay, but the exact setting depends on the procedure, patient and healthcare facility.


### 15. Can both legs be treated at the same time?

In some cases, both legs may be treated, but this depends on the extent of disease, the procedures required and the patient's overall health. Your specialist can advise whether simultaneous treatment is appropriate.


### 16. Is varicose vein treatment covered by insurance?

Insurance coverage depends on your specific policy, the medical necessity of the treatment and the insurer's terms. Ask the hospital or insurer about eligibility before proceeding.


### 17. Can varicose veins be treated during pregnancy?

Interventional treatment is generally not performed during pregnancy except in exceptional circumstances. NICE recommends discussing the effect of pregnancy on varicose veins and considering compression hosiery for symptom relief when appropriate.


### 18. What is the first step if I want treatment without surgery?

The first step is a consultation with an appropriate specialist. A clinical assessment and duplex ultrasound can help identify the underlying venous problem and determine whether minimally invasive treatment is suitable.

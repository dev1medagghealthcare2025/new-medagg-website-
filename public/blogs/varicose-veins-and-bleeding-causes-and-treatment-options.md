# Varicose Veins and Bleeding | Causes & Treatment Options - medagg

Understanding Varicose Bleeding and Veins
-----------------------------------------

**What are Varicose Veins?**
----------------------------

Varicose veins are enlarged, twisted veins that most commonly occur in the legs. Symptoms include visible veins, swelling, and discoloration. These venous system are often seen as a cosmetic concern but they can lead to more serious complications, such as Varicose Bleeding.

Varicose bleeding occurs from ruptured varicose veins. This condition can be alarming because varicose veins are located close to the surface of the skin, making them prone to injury or spontaneous rupture, even from minor trauma.

![](https://delawareadvancedveincenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/3-1024x1024.png)

**Bleeding Varicose Veins: A Serious Complication**
---------------------------------------------------

Fragile skin makes it easy to damage varicose veins, leading to bleeding from the vein.

Bleeding is due to their weakened walls and high blood volume. The swollen nature of these veins means that a slight rupture has occurred that can lead to rapid blood loss.

Venous blood appears dark red due to its deoxygenated state. A large bruise can occur internally, indicating blood spreading under the skin.

A bleeding vein requires immediate attention, such as applying pressure to stop the bleeding. Getting checked out by a doctor helps avoid serious blood loss.

![](https://www.schulmanveincenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/BLEEDING_LEG_VARICOSE_VEINS-657x372.png)

**What Causes the Bleeding**
----------------------------

Weak/damaged valves and bulged veins sit close to the skin surface, making them vulnerable to rupture. Common triggers include:

- Skin injury over thin skin
- Heat exposure (vein dilatation)
- Prolonged standing/sitting (venous pressure)
- Age (reduced elasticity)
- Genetics (family history)
- Pregnancy (hormonal + volume changes)
- Obesity (higher venous pressure)

**Symptoms of Varicose Veins**
------------------------------

- Sudden onset of bleeding; can spurt under pressure
- Visible wound over a varicose vein
- Bleeding difficult to stop without firm pressure
- Signs of blood loss: dizziness, fatigue, fainting (severe cases)

**Types of Bleeding from Varicose Veins**
-----------------------------------------

External bleeding can occur from varicose, reticular, or spider veins and may be significant.

![](https://media.springernature.com/lw1200/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs12024-017-9897-y/MediaObjects/12024_2017_9897_Fig3_HTML.gif)

Intradermal bleeding occurs when a superficial vein ruptures within the skin, creating a blue-purple bruise.

![Bleeding & Hemorhage - Gitter Vein](https://gittervein.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/21-768x336.jpg)

**Why is Varicose Bleeding Dangerous?**
---------------------------------------

- Significant blood loss if not controlled quickly
- Recurrence unless the underlying vein disease is treated
- Infection risk due to an open wound
- Skin ulcers with chronic venous insufficiency

**First Aid: Managing a Bleeding Varicose Vein**
-----------------------------------------------

- Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth/gauze
- Elevate the leg above heart level
- Seek medical attention if not stopped in 10–15 minutes or if bleeding is heavy
- Avoid tight clothing over the site; keep it clean and protected

**Treatment Options to Prevent Rebleeding**
-------------------------------------------

Definitive care targets the source veins to prevent recurrence. Minimally invasive options include:

### Endovenous Ablation (Laser/RFA)

- A catheter delivers heat to close the diseased vein; blood reroutes to healthy veins
- Local anesthesia; walk-in, walk-out; quick recovery

### Ultrasound-Guided Foam Sclerotherapy

- Foam sclerosant displaces blood and seals the vein under ultrasound guidance
- Useful for tortuous or residual surface veins

### Microphlebectomy (Selected cases)

- Tiny incisions to remove bulging segments when appropriate; often adjunct to ablation

**Why Choose Minimally Invasive Care?**

- No large incisions; local anesthesia
- Quick return to routine (often 1–2 days)
- High success, low complication rates

**Prevention Tips**
-------------------

- Maintain healthy weight
- Exercise (walking, swimming, cycling)
- Avoid prolonged sitting/standing; move breaks
- Wear graduated compression stockings as advised
- Manage blood pressure

**When to Seek Medical Attention**
----------------------------------

- Sudden heavy bleeding or recurrent bleeds
- Non-healing wounds or skin color/texture changes
- Significant pain, swelling, or signs of infection

Early evaluation and timely treatment reduce the risk of rebleeding and complications and improve comfort and leg health.

## What Are Uterine Fibroids?

Uterine fibroids are benign growths that develop from the muscular tissue of the uterus. They are also known as leiomyomas or myomas.

Fibroids can vary significantly in:

* Size
* Number
* Location
* Growth pattern

Some women may have a single small fibroid, while others may have multiple fibroids of different sizes.

Fibroids can develop within the wall of the uterus, on the outer surface of the uterus or toward the inside of the uterine cavity.

Not every fibroid requires treatment.

Some women have fibroids without experiencing any symptoms and may simply need monitoring.

However, fibroids can cause significant symptoms in some women.


## What Are the Symptoms of Fibroids?

The symptoms depend largely on the size and location of the fibroids.

Common symptoms include:

* Heavy menstrual bleeding
* Periods lasting longer than usual
* Painful periods
* Pelvic pain or pressure
* Lower back pain
* Abdominal fullness or enlargement
* Needing to urinate more frequently than usual
* A feeling that the bladder does not empty completely
* Constipation
* Pain during sexual intercourse
* Anaemia caused by heavy menstrual bleeding
* Fertility problems in some women

Some women may have no symptoms at all.

If heavy menstrual bleeding is causing anaemia or interfering with daily activities, or if you experience significant pain or pressure, it may be time to discuss treatment options with a specialist.


## Do All Fibroids Need Treatment?


## No.

Treatment is not necessarily required for every fibroid.

Small fibroids that are not causing symptoms may simply be monitored.

Treatment may be considered when fibroids cause problems such as:

* Heavy or painful periods
* Anaemia
* Pelvic pain or pressure
* Pressure-related urinary or bowel symptoms
* Significant abdominal enlargement
* Fertility concerns
* Rapid growth or uncertainty about the diagnosis

The decision to treat depends on several factors, including the size and location of the fibroids and your personal treatment goals.


## Can Fibroids Be Treated Without Surgery?


## In some women, yes.

There are several approaches that may be considered depending on the individual situation.

These can include:


### 1. Observation and monitoring

If fibroids are small and not causing significant symptoms, your doctor may recommend monitoring them rather than immediately treating them.


### 2. Medication

Certain medications can help control symptoms such as heavy menstrual bleeding and painful periods.

However, medication does not necessarily eliminate the fibroids themselves.

For example, some hormonal treatments can control bleeding, while certain GnRH medications can temporarily shrink fibroids. After some medications are stopped, fibroids may grow back toward their previous size.


### 3. Uterine Artery Embolization

Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE), also called Uterine Fibroid Embolization (UFE), is a minimally invasive treatment that reduces the blood supply to fibroids, causing them to shrink over time.

This is one of the important alternatives to surgical treatment for appropriately selected women.


## What Is Uterine Fibroid Embolization?

Uterine Fibroid Embolization is a minimally invasive procedure performed by an interventional radiologist.

Instead of surgically removing the fibroids, the procedure targets the blood vessels supplying them.

A catheter is introduced through a small access point in an artery, and tiny embolization particles are delivered into the blood vessels supplying the fibroids.

These particles reduce the blood flow to the fibroids.

Over time, the fibroids shrink and symptoms may improve.

The procedure is designed to treat the fibroids while preserving the uterus.


## How Does Uterine Fibroid Embolization Work?

The procedure generally follows these steps:


### Step 1: Evaluation

Your medical history, symptoms and imaging results are reviewed to determine whether embolization may be appropriate.


### Step 2: Imaging

Ultrasound and, when needed, other imaging can help determine the size, number and location of the fibroids.


### Step 3: Catheter Placement

A small catheter is inserted into an artery, commonly through the groin or another suitable access site.


### Step 4: Reaching the Uterine Arteries

The catheter is guided toward the arteries supplying the uterus using imaging guidance.


### Step 5: Embolization

Small particles are delivered into the blood vessels supplying the fibroids.

This reduces their blood supply.


### Step 6: Fibroid Shrinkage

The treated fibroids gradually shrink over time, which can lead to improvement in symptoms.


## Is Uterine Fibroid Embolization the Same as Surgery?


## No.

Uterine fibroid embolization is a minimally invasive procedure rather than traditional open surgery.

A catheter is used to access the blood vessels instead of making a large abdominal incision to remove the fibroids.

ACOG notes that UAE can be used instead of hysterectomy or myomectomy for fibroid treatment in appropriate patients.


## However, "non-surgical" does not mean "risk-free."

Like any medical procedure, embolization has potential risks and should only be considered after an appropriate medical evaluation.


## UFE vs Hysterectomy

One of the biggest concerns women have after being diagnosed with fibroids is:


## "Will I need a hysterectomy?"

A hysterectomy removes the uterus and means pregnancy is no longer possible.

Uterine artery embolization, on the other hand, is designed to treat the fibroids while preserving the uterus.

| Uterine Fibroid Embolization | Hysterectomy |
| --- | --- |
| Minimally invasive procedure | Surgical procedure |
| Uterus is preserved | Uterus is removed |
| Fibroids are treated by reducing their blood supply | Uterus containing the fibroids is removed |
| Generally shorter recovery than major abdominal surgery | Recovery depends on surgical approach |
| Pregnancy considerations require individual specialist discussion | Pregnancy is not possible after hysterectomy |
| Additional treatment may sometimes be required | Definitive removal of the uterus |

The right choice depends on the woman's medical condition, symptoms, age and reproductive goals.


## UFE vs Myomectomy

Another treatment option is myomectomy, which involves surgically removing the fibroids while keeping the uterus.

Myomectomy may be appropriate for some women, particularly depending on the number, size and location of their fibroids.

UFE takes a different approach by reducing the blood supply to the fibroids rather than surgically removing them.

ACOG notes that myomectomy and UAE are different treatment approaches and that the choice depends on factors such as the woman's condition and treatment goals.

If preserving fertility is a priority, it is particularly important to discuss your pregnancy plans with your gynaecologist and interventional radiologist before deciding on treatment.


## Who May Be Suitable for Uterine Fibroid Embolization?

UAE may be considered for women who have symptomatic fibroids and are looking for a minimally invasive treatment option.

It may be particularly relevant for women who:

* Have heavy menstrual bleeding
* Have pelvic pressure or pain
* Have symptomatic fibroids
* Have multiple fibroids
* Want to avoid a hysterectomy
* Want to preserve the uterus
* Have been advised to consider surgery
* Are looking for a minimally invasive alternative

However, not every woman with fibroids is a candidate for UAE.

Your specialist needs to evaluate your individual case before recommending the procedure.


## What Tests Are Needed Before Fibroid Treatment?

Your doctor may recommend imaging and other investigations depending on your symptoms.

These may include:


### Ultrasound

Ultrasound is commonly used to identify fibroids and assess their size and location.


### MRI

In some cases, MRI may provide additional information about the uterus and fibroids and help with treatment planning.


### Blood Tests

If you have heavy menstrual bleeding, blood tests may be recommended to check for anaemia.


### Gynaecological Evaluation

A gynaecologist may assess your symptoms, reproductive plans and overall health before treatment.

The exact tests required depend on your individual situation.


## What Is the Recovery After Uterine Fibroid Embolization?

Recovery varies from person to person.

Some patients may return home on the same day, while others may stay overnight depending on their condition and the treating centre. ACOG notes that UAE is often performed as an outpatient procedure, although some women may stay in hospital overnight.

After the procedure, you may experience:

* Pelvic cramps
* Abdominal pain
* Fatigue
* Mild fever
* Nausea
* Discomfort around the catheter access site

Pain and cramping can occur during the initial recovery period.

ACOG notes that abdominal pain and cramps may continue for up to approximately two weeks in some women.

Your doctor will provide specific instructions regarding medication, activity and follow-up.


## When Will Fibroids Start Shrinking After UFE?

Fibroids do not disappear immediately after embolization.

The process occurs gradually.

Symptoms may begin improving over the following weeks and months.

ACOG notes that it can take approximately 2 to 3 months to see a change in symptoms, while fibroids may continue shrinking for a year or longer.

Follow-up imaging may be recommended to assess the response to treatment.


## Does Uterine Fibroid Embolization Affect Fertility?

This is one of the most important questions to discuss before treatment.

If you are planning to become pregnant, tell your gynaecologist and interventional radiologist before considering UAE.

ACOG states that the effect of UAE on future pregnancy is not completely clear, and NICE recommends discussing the uncertainty around fertility and pregnancy during the consent process.

Therefore, women who are actively planning pregnancy should have an individual fertility-focused discussion before choosing a treatment.


## Can Fibroids Come Back After UFE?

UFE can reduce the size of treated fibroids and improve symptoms, but it is not a guarantee that fibroids will never cause problems again.

Some women may experience recurrent symptoms or require additional treatment.

NICE advises patients considering UAE that symptoms may not be relieved in every case, may return, and may sometimes require further treatment.

## Why Consider Fibroid Treatment Without Surgery?

For an appropriately selected patient, minimally invasive treatment may provide an alternative to traditional surgery.

Potential advantages of UFE/UAE include:

* No abdominal incision for fibroid removal
* Uterus preservation
* Ability to treat multiple fibroids
* Shorter hospital stay in many cases
* Earlier return to normal activities compared with some surgical approaches

ACOG notes that UAE can treat multiple fibroids and typically involves a shorter hospital stay and earlier return to activities compared with hysterectomy or myomectomy.

However, the benefits and risks must be considered for each individual patient.

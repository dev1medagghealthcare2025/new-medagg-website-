## What Is a Varicocele?

A varicocele is an enlargement of the veins within the scrotum that drain blood away from the testicle.

It is similar in principle to varicose veins that develop in the legs.

When the valves or blood-flow pathways in these veins do not work properly, blood can reflux and pool within the veins. Over time, the veins can become enlarged.

Some men have a varicocele without any symptoms and may not require treatment.

Others may experience:

* Dull or aching testicular pain
* A feeling of heaviness in the scrotum
* Swelling
* Enlarged or prominent veins
* Discomfort that becomes worse after standing or physical activity
* Testicular size changes
* Fertility problems

Varicoceles have also been associated with male infertility in some men.


## Do All Varicoceles Need Treatment?


## No.

A varicocele does not automatically mean that treatment is required.

The decision depends on factors such as:

* Whether you have pain or discomfort
* Whether the varicocele is clinically significant
* Testicular health
* Semen-analysis results
* Fertility goals
* Age
* Whether the varicocele has returned after previous treatment
* Findings from ultrasound or Doppler examination

For men being evaluated for infertility, current WHO guidance supports considering treatment for a clinical varicocele with abnormal semen parameters in appropriate patients who are trying to conceive. The guideline recognises both surgical and radiological treatment, including embolization.


## Can Varicocele Be Treated Without Surgery?

Yes. Varicocele embolization is a minimally invasive treatment option that does not require conventional surgery.

Instead of making an incision to surgically tie off the affected veins, an interventional radiologist uses a thin catheter to reach the abnormal veins and block them.

Once the abnormal veins are blocked, blood is redirected through healthier veins.

Over time, the enlarged veins can shrink and the varicocele can become less prominent.


## What Is Varicocele Embolization?

Varicocele embolization is an image-guided procedure used to block the abnormal veins responsible for a varicocele.

It is performed by an interventional radiologist.

Unlike conventional varicocele surgery, the treatment does not require an incision in the scrotum or groin to surgically tie the veins.

A small catheter is introduced through a blood vessel, commonly through the groin or neck depending on the technique used. The catheter is guided toward the abnormal testicular vein using X-ray imaging.

The vein is then blocked using an embolic material such as coils or another appropriate agent.

This stops abnormal blood flow through the affected vein.


## How Does Varicocele Embolization Work?

The procedure generally involves several steps.


### Step 1: Consultation and Assessment

Your doctor will evaluate your symptoms, medical history and treatment goals.

If fertility is a concern, a semen analysis may be part of the evaluation.


### Step 2: Imaging

An ultrasound with Doppler may be used to assess the testicles and veins and identify abnormal blood flow.


### Step 3: Catheter Placement

A small catheter is inserted into a blood vessel.


### Step 4: Reaching the Abnormal Vein

Using real-time X-ray imaging, the interventional radiologist guides the catheter to the affected vein.


### Step 5: Embolization

The abnormal vein is blocked using an appropriate embolic material.


### Step 6: Completion

Once the abnormal blood flow has been addressed, the catheter is removed and the small access site is closed.

The goal is to stop abnormal venous reflux while allowing blood to continue draining through healthy veins.


## Is Varicocele Embolization Really Non-Surgical?

Yes.

Varicocele embolization is considered a radiological or minimally invasive treatment, rather than conventional open surgery.

There is no surgical incision made to access and tie off the enlarged veins.

The procedure is performed through a small vascular access point using a catheter and imaging guidance.

However, minimally invasive does not mean risk-free.

As with any medical procedure, embolization has potential risks and should be recommended only after an appropriate evaluation.


## Varicocele Embolization vs Varicocele Surgery

Both approaches aim to stop abnormal blood flow through the veins responsible for the varicocele.

The main difference is how the veins are treated.

| Varicocele Embolization | Varicocelectomy |
| --- | --- |
| Minimally invasive radiological procedure | Surgical procedure |
| Performed by an interventional radiologist | Usually performed by a urologist |
| Uses a catheter to reach the abnormal vein | Uses a surgical approach to access the affected veins |
| No conventional surgical incision in the groin/scrotum | Requires a surgical incision depending on technique |
| Uses embolic material to block abnormal veins | Enlarged veins are surgically ligated |
| Often performed as a day procedure | Often performed as an outpatient procedure |
| Recovery is generally relatively short | Recovery varies by surgical technique |

Both surgical and radiological approaches are recognised treatment options in appropriate patients. The choice should be based on the individual's condition, available expertise and personal preference.


## Is Varicocele Embolization Better Than Surgery?

There is no single treatment that is automatically better for every patient.

Both embolization and surgery can be used to treat varicoceles.

Embolization may be attractive to some patients because it avoids conventional surgery and uses a catheter-based approach.

Surgery may be preferred in other situations depending on the anatomy, previous treatment, clinical findings and available expertise.

The right question is not:


## "Which treatment is better for everyone?"

It is:


## "Which treatment is appropriate for my varicocele?"

A specialist evaluation can help answer that question.


## Can Varicocele Embolization Improve Fertility?

This is one of the most important questions for men diagnosed with varicocele.

Varicoceles can be associated with impaired semen parameters and male infertility.

Treatment is sometimes considered when a man has a clinical varicocele, infertility and abnormal semen parameters.

Current WHO guidance recognises both surgical and radiological treatment for appropriately selected men with infertility and clinical varicocele. The guideline also notes that patients should understand that the effect of treatment on live birth outcomes remains uncertain.

Treatment should therefore not be promoted as a guarantee of pregnancy.

If fertility is your primary concern, your evaluation may include:

* Physical examination
* Scrotal ultrasound/Doppler
* Semen analysis
* Medical and fertility history
* Assessment of other potential causes of infertility

Your doctor can then determine whether varicocele treatment may be appropriate.


## Can Varicocele Embolization Improve Sperm Count?

In appropriately selected men, treatment of a clinical varicocele can be followed by improvements in semen parameters.

The goal of treatment is to stop abnormal venous reflux and improve the conditions around the testicle.

However, results vary from person to person.

A semen analysis before treatment and appropriate follow-up after treatment can help assess changes in sperm concentration, motility and other parameters.

Treatment should therefore be viewed as one part of a fertility evaluation, rather than a guaranteed solution to infertility.

## Can Varicocele Return After Embolization?

A varicocele can recur or persist after treatment.

This can happen if some abnormal veins remain open or if new abnormal blood-flow pathways develop.

The possibility of recurrence should be discussed before treatment.

Follow-up is particularly important when the original reason for treatment was infertility, pain or another significant symptom.


## What Are the Risks of Varicocele Embolization?

Although varicocele embolization is minimally invasive, it still has potential risks.

Possible complications can include:

* Bleeding or bruising at the catheter access site
* Infection
* Temporary pain or discomfort
* Reaction to contrast material
* Migration of embolic material
* Persistence or recurrence of the varicocele
* Failure to achieve the intended result

The exact risks depend on the technique used and your individual circumstances.

Your doctor should explain the potential benefits and risks before you consent to treatment.

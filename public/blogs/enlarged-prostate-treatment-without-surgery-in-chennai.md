# Enlarged Prostate Treatment Without Surgery in Chennai

Are you waking up several times at night to urinate? Finding it difficult to start urinating? Or experiencing a weak urine stream?

These can be common symptoms of an enlarged prostate, also known as benign prostatic enlargement (BPE) or benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH).

If you have been told that your enlarged prostate may eventually require surgery, you may be wondering:

In many cases, treatment does not begin with surgery.

## What Is an Enlarged Prostate?

The prostate is a gland located below the bladder and around part of the urethra, the tube through which urine leaves the body.

As men age, the prostate can gradually become enlarged.

When the prostate grows, it can press against or narrow the urethra, making it harder for urine to pass.

This condition is commonly referred to as benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) or benign prostate enlargement.

Importantly, benign prostate enlargement is not prostate cancer.

However, similar urinary symptoms can occur with other prostate or urinary conditions, which is why proper medical evaluation is important.


## What Are the Symptoms of an Enlarged Prostate?

An enlarged prostate can cause lower urinary tract symptoms such as:

* Weak urine stream
* Difficulty starting urination
* Straining to urinate
* Frequent urination
* Urgency to urinate
* Waking up multiple times at night to urinate
* Feeling that the bladder has not completely emptied
* A urine stream that starts and stops
* Dribbling after urination
* Difficulty maintaining a normal urine flow

Symptoms can range from mild to severe.

Some men may have an enlarged prostate with relatively few symptoms, while others may experience significant disruption to sleep, work and everyday life.


## Does Every Man With an Enlarged Prostate Need Treatment?


## No.

If symptoms are mild and not significantly affecting your quality of life, a doctor may recommend monitoring, lifestyle changes and regular follow-up rather than immediately starting a procedure.

European Association of Urology guidance notes that men with mild or non-bothersome symptoms may be managed conservatively through monitoring, lifestyle changes and self-care.

Treatment may become necessary when symptoms:

* Interfere with sleep
* Affect daily activities
* Make travelling difficult
* Cause significant urinary discomfort
* Become progressively worse
* Do not respond adequately to medication
* Lead to complications

The right treatment depends on the individual patient.


## Can an Enlarged Prostate Be Treated Without Surgery?


## Yes, in some men.

There are several ways to manage an enlarged prostate without conventional surgery.

The treatment options may include:


### 1. Lifestyle and Behavioural Changes

For men with mild symptoms, certain changes may help manage urinary symptoms.

These may include:

* Reducing fluids before bedtime
* Limiting excessive caffeine
* Limiting alcohol, particularly if it worsens urinary symptoms
* Reviewing medicines with your doctor if they may be contributing to urinary symptoms
* Maintaining a healthy body weight
* Managing constipation
* Practising appropriate bladder habits

These measures may help with symptoms but do not physically remove or shrink the prostate.


### 2. Medicines

Medication is another common treatment for benign prostate enlargement.

Depending on the patient's symptoms and prostate characteristics, doctors may prescribe medicines that:

* Relax the muscles around the prostate and bladder neck
* Improve urine flow
* Reduce prostate size over time
* Reduce the risk of urinary retention in selected patients

The European Association of Urology notes that medication selection depends on factors including symptom severity, prostate size, overall health and patient preferences.

Medication does not work equally well for everyone.

Some men continue to experience significant symptoms despite medication.


### 3. Prostate Artery Embolization

For appropriately selected men looking for a minimally invasive alternative to conventional prostate surgery, one option is Prostate Artery Embolization (PAE).

PAE is performed by an interventional radiologist using image guidance.

Instead of surgically removing prostate tissue, the procedure reduces the blood supply to portions of the enlarged prostate.

Over time, reduced blood flow can cause the prostate tissue to shrink, potentially improving urinary symptoms.

The EAU describes PAE as a minimally invasive treatment option for men with moderate-to-severe lower urinary tract symptoms who wish to consider a minimally invasive approach and accept that outcomes may be less optimal than TURP.


## What Is Prostate Artery Embolization?

Prostate Artery Embolization (PAE) is a catheter-based procedure used to treat urinary symptoms associated with benign prostate enlargement.

The procedure is performed by an interventional radiologist.

A thin catheter is introduced through an artery, commonly through the wrist or groin.

Using imaging guidance, the catheter is carefully navigated to the arteries supplying the prostate.

Tiny embolization particles are then delivered into the appropriate prostate arteries.

This reduces blood flow to the enlarged prostate tissue.

Over time, the prostate may shrink, which can help relieve pressure around the urethra and improve urinary symptoms.


## How Does Prostate Artery Embolization Work?

The procedure generally involves the following steps.


### Step 1: Consultation

Your symptoms, medical history, medications and previous prostate treatments are reviewed.


### Step 2: Prostate Evaluation

Your doctor may recommend tests to assess prostate size, urinary function and the cause of your symptoms.


### Step 3: Imaging

Imaging may be used to assess the prostate and its blood supply and to plan the procedure.


### Step 4: Catheter Placement

A thin catheter is introduced through a small vascular access point.


### Step 5: Reaching the Prostate Arteries

Using X-ray guidance, the interventional radiologist navigates the catheter to the arteries supplying the prostate.


### Step 6: Embolization

Tiny particles are released into the selected arteries to reduce blood flow to the enlarged prostate tissue.


### Step 7: Recovery

The catheter is removed and the access site is monitored.

Depending on the individual case and treatment centre, PAE can be performed as a day procedure. The procedure may be performed through an artery in the groin or wrist, depending on the patient’s anatomy and the interventional radiologist’s approach.


## Is Prostate Artery Embolization a Surgery?


## No.

PAE is a minimally invasive, image-guided procedure rather than conventional prostate surgery.

It does not involve surgically cutting away the prostate through the urethra or making a large abdominal incision.

However, "non-surgical" does not mean risk-free.

PAE involves blood-vessel access, contrast imaging and embolization, so it should only be performed by an appropriately trained team after determining that the procedure is suitable.

PAE is best planned through a multidisciplinary approach, with urologists and trained interventional radiologists working together to assess suitability and provide appropriate follow-up.


## PAE vs TURP: What's the Difference?

TURP, or Transurethral Resection of the Prostate, is a well-established surgical treatment for BPH. It removes prostate tissue that is obstructing urine flow.

PAE works differently by reducing the blood supply to the prostate.

| Prostate Artery Embolization | TURP |
| --- | --- |
| Minimally invasive catheter-based procedure | Surgical procedure |
| Performed by an interventional radiologist | Performed by a urologist |
| Uses arterial access | Uses an instrument passed through the urethra |
| Does not surgically remove prostate tissue | Removes obstructing prostate tissue |
| Can be performed as a day procedure in appropriate settings | Usually requires a hospital procedure |
| Recovery can be relatively short | Recovery varies depending on the patient |
| May have a lower impact on some sexual-function outcomes | Can have sexual and urinary side effects |
| May have a higher chance of needing retreatment | Generally more durable for obstruction |

However, PAE is not simply a replacement for TURP in every patient.

The EAU states that PAE can provide a minimally invasive option but that men should understand that outcomes may be less optimal than TURP.

Evidence suggests that PAE may offer advantages in certain safety and sexual-function outcomes, although it may provide less improvement than standard surgical treatments for some urinary-function measures.

The best option depends on your prostate size, symptoms, medical history, urinary function and treatment preferences.


## Who May Consider Prostate Artery Embolization?

PAE may be considered for appropriately evaluated men who:

* Have bothersome urinary symptoms due to benign prostate enlargement
* Have moderate-to-severe symptoms
* Have not achieved adequate relief from medication
* Prefer a minimally invasive treatment
* Want to avoid conventional prostate surgery
* May have reasons that make conventional surgery less desirable
* Understand that PAE may not provide the same functional improvement as some surgical treatments

The EAU specifically recommends PAE as an option for men with moderate-to-severe symptoms who want a minimally invasive treatment and accept the possibility of less optimal outcomes compared with TURP.


## Is PAE Suitable for Everyone?


## No.

A proper evaluation is essential before deciding on PAE.

Your doctor may consider:

* Severity of urinary symptoms
* Prostate size
* Prostate anatomy
* Urinary flow
* Post-void residual urine
* Current medications
* Previous prostate procedures
* Blood-vessel anatomy
* Other medical conditions
* Whether your symptoms are actually caused by prostate enlargement

PAE should not be considered simply because a man wants to avoid surgery.

The underlying cause of the urinary symptoms must first be established.


## What Tests Are Needed Before Enlarged Prostate Treatment?

Depending on your symptoms, your doctor may recommend several evaluations.


### Medical History

Your doctor will ask about:

* Urinary symptoms
* Night-time urination
* Previous prostate problems
* Current medicines
* Previous urinary procedures


### Physical Examination

A prostate examination may be recommended depending on your symptoms and medical history.


### Urine Tests

Urine testing can help identify infections or other urinary problems.


### PSA Testing

Your doctor may recommend a PSA blood test depending on your age, symptoms and prostate-cancer risk.


### Ultrasound

Ultrasound can provide information about prostate size and bladder emptying.


### Urinary Flow Testing

Uroflowmetry can help assess how effectively urine is flowing.


### Post-Void Residual Measurement

This measures how much urine remains in the bladder after urination.


### Imaging for PAE

If PAE is being considered, additional imaging may be required to evaluate the prostate and its blood supply and plan the procedure.

Your doctor will decide which tests are appropriate for you.


## What Is the Recovery After Prostate Artery Embolization?

One of the potential advantages of PAE is that it can be performed as a minimally invasive procedure, with relatively short hospitalisation in appropriate cases.

The EAU notes that catheterisation and hospitalisation time are favourable for PAE compared with TURP.

After the procedure, you may experience temporary symptoms such as:

* Mild pelvic discomfort
* Increased urinary frequency
* Burning while urinating
* Blood in the urine
* Fatigue
* Temporary worsening of urinary symptoms

These symptoms are not experienced by everyone and can vary in severity.

Your medical team will provide specific instructions regarding medicines, activity and follow-up.


## How Quickly Does PAE Improve Urinary Symptoms?

PAE does not work by immediately removing prostate tissue.

The treatment works gradually by reducing the blood supply to the prostate, after which the prostate can shrink.

Therefore, improvement may occur progressively rather than immediately after the procedure.

Your doctor will explain what improvement to expect based on your individual condition.

Follow-up is important to determine whether symptoms are improving and whether additional treatment is required.


## Can Prostate Artery Embolization Reduce Prostate Size?

PAE is intended to reduce blood flow to enlarged prostate tissue, which can result in prostate shrinkage over time.

However, the amount of shrinkage varies between patients.

The main goal of treatment is not simply to reduce prostate size but to improve bothersome urinary symptoms and quality of life.


## Does PAE Affect Sexual Function?

Sexual function is an important consideration when choosing treatment for an enlarged prostate.

Evidence suggests that PAE may have advantages in some sexual-function outcomes compared with certain surgical treatments, although individual results vary. A systematic review found better patient-reported erectile-function outcomes after PAE compared with standard surgical therapies in the studies reviewed.

However, no treatment should be promoted as having zero risk of sexual side effects.

Discuss your priorities regarding erections and ejaculation with your doctor before choosing a treatment.


## Can PAE Help Men Who Want to Avoid TURP?

For appropriately selected patients, yes, PAE can be considered as a minimally invasive alternative to TURP.

However, the trade-off is important.

PAE may offer advantages such as less invasive treatment and shorter hospitalisation, but the EAU guideline states that men considering PAE should accept that outcomes may be less optimal than TURP.

Therefore, the decision should be based on an informed discussion rather than simply choosing the treatment that sounds less invasive.


## What Are the Risks of Prostate Artery Embolization?

PAE is minimally invasive, but it is not risk-free.

Potential complications can include:

* Bleeding or bruising at the catheter access site
* Infection
* Pain or discomfort
* Urinary symptoms during recovery
* Blood in urine or semen
* Contrast-related complications
* Non-target embolization
* Persistent symptoms
* Recurrence of symptoms
* Need for additional treatment

Because prostate blood vessels can have complex anatomy, careful imaging and experienced operators are important.

NICE's evidence review highlights the importance of careful evaluation of target vessels and the risk of non-target embolization.


## Can an Enlarged Prostate Be Treated With Medication Instead?

Yes.

Medication is often considered before an invasive procedure, particularly when symptoms are manageable.

Different medications work in different ways.

Some relax the muscles around the prostate and bladder neck to improve urine flow.

Others can reduce prostate size over time.

Combination treatment may also be used in selected patients.

However, medication is not suitable or effective for every man.

If symptoms remain troublesome despite medication, your doctor may discuss procedural treatment options.


## What Happens If an Enlarged Prostate Is Left Untreated?

Some men with mild symptoms may be safely monitored.

However, worsening prostate-related urinary obstruction can lead to problems such as:

* Increasing difficulty urinating
* Urinary retention
* Recurrent urinary infections
* Bladder problems
* Bladder stones
* Kidney-related complications in severe cases

This does not mean every enlarged prostate will lead to these complications.

The important point is to have persistent or worsening urinary symptoms evaluated rather than ignoring them.
